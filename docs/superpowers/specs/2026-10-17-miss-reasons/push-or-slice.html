Push or slice? You only need one extra fact

Where a shot finishes = where it started + how much it curved. The panel already records the finish (missed right), so the only question is where did it start?. The curve follows from those two.

Finished right of the green. Where did it start?

On line → it curved right
Slice — the clubface

Right, flew straight
Push — path or alignment

Right, kept going right
Push-slice — both

"I don't know where it started" is a valid answer too. You keep today's data and the coach doesn't guess.

Three ways to answer it on the course

1 · Two taps on the tracer

Tap ① where it finished, tap ② where it started on a wide band above the launch. The app draws the flight and shows its name at the top. No drawing skill, so nothing to misread.

Tap ① replaces today's finish grid, so a missed green costs one tap more than today.

2 · Freehand that snaps

Draw freely. While your finger is down, the app snaps the line to the nearest of 9 flights and shows its name live. If it's wrong, keep moving until it's right, then lift.

Most fun, but still needs a careful finger in the first third of the stroke, and sun glare plus gloves make that harder.

3 · Pick the tracer

Nine small tracer drawings and you tap the one that looks like your shot. You recognise it, you don't produce it. Rows are the start line, columns the curve.

Nine shapes is more to scan, and the finish still needs its own tap. Fine for a review screen, slower on the course.

My lean

1 · Two taps. It asks exactly the one question that separates push from slice, as a big target that's easy to hit with a glove. Tap ① replaces today's finish grid, so the extra cost is a single tap on the start band, and you can skip that tap whenever you didn't see the start. Contact (heavy, thin…) and situation (below feet…) stay as chips under the tracer.
